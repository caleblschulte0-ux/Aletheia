# Aletheia — Fluid Opportunity Pursuit

His brief, 2026-09-21, in his words. It is the ruling for how she pursues
an opportunity; the ledger of what was built against it is at the end.

## The principle

Do not build a job-application workflow.

Do not build a fixed sequence of:

find → tailor → apply → message recruiter → follow up.

Do not create a menu of predefined pursuit strategies and merely classify
each job into one.

That is still hardcoding the solution.

Instead, give Aletheia an objective:

For every worthwhile opportunity, use the information available — and
obtain additional information when useful — to determine what actions are
most likely to increase Caleb's chance of getting serious consideration,
while remaining truthful, non-spammy, safe, and within his authority rules.

Every job is its own problem.

## Treat each job as a live situation

When Aletheia finds an opportunity, she should build an understanding of
that specific situation.

That understanding can include anything relevant she already has or can
legitimately obtain:

* the complete posting;
* company website;
* company size and stage;
* what the company sells;
* recent company developments;
* department/team information;
* public employee information;
* recruiter/hiring-manager information;
* likely reporting structure;
* role seniority;
* compensation;
* location;
* applicant requirements;
* unusual wording in the posting;
* technologies/products/markets mentioned;
* Caleb's resume;
* his actual experience;
* his strongest relevant accomplishments;
* previous applications to that company;
* previous interactions with people there;
* publicly available information about the hiring process;
* whether the opening appears new, old, reposted or urgent;
* whether there are several related openings;
* whether someone in Caleb's network creates a legitimate connection;
* what previous job-search tactics have actually produced replies for Caleb.

This list is not the schema.

It is examples of evidence.

Do not limit reasoning to these fields.

If some other information would materially change what Aletheia should
do, she should be able to obtain it and use it.

## Reason job by job

For each opportunity, ask:

Given everything I currently know about Caleb, this role, this company and
the people involved, what is the smartest way to pursue this particular
opportunity?

Then reason.

Maybe the answer is simply applying.

Maybe it is changing which experience is emphasized before applying.

Maybe there is a particularly strong hiring manager worth contacting.

Maybe there is no identifiable hiring manager, but a recruiter has publicly
posted about the opening.

Maybe an employee attended the same university.

Maybe Caleb has unusually relevant MCA/fintech experience and a short note
explaining that connection would matter.

Maybe the company is entering a market Caleb understands and a short piece
of thoughtful analysis would demonstrate something valuable.

Maybe a portfolio/artifact makes sense.

Maybe making one would look ridiculous.

Maybe the best move is contacting someone before applying.

Maybe the best move is applying immediately because the role is fresh and
doing research afterward.

Maybe there are several positions at the same employer and Aletheia should
decide which one presents the strongest path rather than blindly applying
to all of them.

Maybe further action would add no value and the correct move is to submit
the application and leave it alone.

Maybe the opening is technically a fit but poor enough that Caleb's time
should not be spent on it at all.

There is no required sequence.

## Information gathering is part of reasoning

Aletheia should not make the entire decision from the job description if
additional information could materially improve it.

She should be able to think:

"I don't know enough yet to decide whether outreach is worthwhile."

and then investigate.

Or:

"This appears unusually promising. Before applying, I want to understand
what this team is building."

Then research it.

Or:

"I found the VP this role reports to, but there is no evidence she is
involved in hiring. Messaging her would probably be noise."

Then don't.

The decision loop is:

observe → understand → identify uncertainty → gather useful evidence →
reason → act → observe the result → update the plan

Not:

run job workflow.

## Effort should emerge from the opportunity

Do not assign arbitrary fixed tiers like:

* bronze;
* silver;
* gold.

Do not say every "high-fit" job receives five extra actions.

The appropriate amount of work should emerge from the evidence.

Aletheia can reasonably conclude:

"This is worth twenty minutes of additional work."

or:

"There is nothing else useful to do here."

or:

"This opportunity is unusually important; further investigation is
justified."

The amount of effort is itself a decision.

## Actions are tools, not workflow stages

Aletheia may have general abilities such as:

* research;
* browser use;
* document editing;
* writing;
* contact discovery;
* email;
* professional-network interaction where permitted;
* scheduling;
* reminders;
* follow-up;
* creating artifacts;
* repository/project work;
* monitoring for replies.

Those capabilities should remain general.

The job pursuit reasoner decides whether any of them are useful for this
opportunity.

Do not encode:

after application -> find recruiter

Instead:

goal: improve this opportunity's chance of progressing
available evidence: ...
available capabilities: ...
what, if anything, is the most useful next action?

That distinction is critical.

## Keep asking "why would this help?"

Before doing an optional action, Aletheia should be able to state the
causal reason.

Example:

"The posting specifically emphasizes lender partnerships. Caleb currently
manages difficult funding-partner relationships, so a short direct note to
the partnerships VP could expose a highly relevant part of his background
that the standard application may bury."

That has a reason.

This does not:

"We message hiring managers after applications."

If Aletheia cannot explain why an action may improve this particular
opportunity, don't do it merely because the capability exists.

## Stand out intelligently, not theatrically

"Stand out" does not mean doing the most elaborate thing possible.

It means finding real asymmetry.

Ask:

What does Caleb legitimately have, know, understand, or demonstrate that
the normal applicant may not?

That could be:

* unusual industry knowledge;
* direct experience with the company's problem;
* a relationship;
* knowledge of its customer base;
* a project;
* evidence of initiative;
* a concrete idea;
* relevant operational experience;
* familiarity with a market;
* an unusually good reason for the move.

Surface that.

Do not fabricate differentiation.

Do not generate gimmicks just because AI can generate them.

## Aletheia may invent tactics, not facts

This is important.

Thea should be allowed to create new strategies that nobody explicitly
programmed.

If available tools and evidence support a smart move, she should be able
to reason her way to it.

For example, she might discover that a target company recently announced a
new lending product and independently conclude:

"Caleb's current experience managing MCA funding partners is particularly
relevant to this launch. A concise note connecting those two facts may be
stronger than a generic cover letter."

Nobody should have needed to program:

    if company_launched_lending_product:
        message_hiring_manager()

The model discovered the relationship.

That is the behavior we want.

But factual claims about Caleb or the company must remain grounded in
evidence.

Creative strategy is encouraged. Creative biography is forbidden.

## Plans are temporary

Aletheia's plan for an opportunity is not a workflow record carved in
stone.

It is her current best hypothesis.

New information can change it.

If a recruiter replies: change the plan.

If someone refers Caleb: change the plan.

If the role closes: stop.

If another position at the company appears to fit substantially better:
reconsider.

If outreach gets ignored: decide whether another action has expected value
rather than blindly executing "follow-up #2."

If Caleb gets an interview: the objective changes from getting noticed to
maximizing interview performance.

The opportunity persists.

The strategy evolves.

## Learn from outcomes without turning learning into rigid rules

Track what actually happens:

application, response, recruiter contact, interview, rejection, offer,
silence, timing and the actions taken.

Use those outcomes as evidence for future reasoning.

But avoid rules like:

"Hiring-manager messages produce 13% more responses, therefore always
message hiring managers."

Context matters.

The useful learning is closer to:

"Direct outreach has worked particularly well when Caleb has a concrete
industry connection to mention, but generic recruiter messages have rarely
produced responses."

That becomes context for the next decision, not an immutable workflow.

## The durable object is the opportunity

The thing Aletheia carries should not be an application.

It should be something closer to:

Opportunity

* objective;
* evidence;
* current understanding;
* uncertainty;
* actions already taken;
* effects observed;
* commitments made;
* things that must not be repeated;
* people involved;
* next decision point;
* current strategy;
* outcome.

Do not force every opportunity to populate the same giant structure.

Store enough durable evidence that another reasoning pass can understand
what has happened and decide what to do next.

## The test

A developer should not be able to answer:

"What does Aletheia always do after applying?"

There should be no answer other than:

"She looks at that specific opportunity and decides whether anything else
is worth doing."

Likewise:

"When does she contact a hiring manager?"

Answer:

"When the available evidence gives her a reason to believe that is a
useful move for that particular opportunity."

"When does she make an artifact?"

"When she reasons that a particular artifact could demonstrate something
materially useful for that particular opportunity."

"How much effort does she spend?"

"As much as the expected value of the opportunity and additional action
justifies."

## The end state

Caleb says:

"Thea, find me a better job."

That is enough.

Aletheia finds opportunities.

One receives a normal application.

One receives a substantially tailored application because Caleb's
experience maps unusually well.

One gets skipped after deeper research reveals a bad fit.

For another, she discovers a person worth contacting and writes a genuinely
specific message.

For another, she realizes a short analysis demonstrating Caleb's knowledge
would be valuable and creates it.

Another is left alone after applying because additional outreach would
probably just create noise.

Another becomes much more interesting after new company information is
discovered, so she spends additional effort.

Caleb did not specify any of this.

Neither did the programmer.

Aletheia looked at each situation, acquired useful information, reasoned
about what would improve the outcome, and acted accordingly.

That is fluid.

That is the standard.

## What was built against it

(kept in order, newest first)

- **2026-09-21 — the opportunity is the durable object** (`aletheia/pursuit.py`,
  `aletheia/pursuit_applications.py`). What existed ended at "submitted": an
  employer's reply was classified and notified and then forgotten, nothing
  wrote an outcome unless he said it out loud, and nothing after the form
  was ever decided. Now every live application is opened ONCE as an
  opportunity carrying its evidence (the record, the posting, his
  background, his own facts, his history with that employer), and a pass
  over one asks the reasoner what, if anything, would help — with a catalog
  of general moves (look, submit, note to a person, write, wait, leave,
  close) and no order among them. A move must say why it would help THIS
  opportunity and cite the evidence it rests on, or it is dropped; a note or
  a document must name the evidence its claims stand on; a kind she has no
  tool for becomes a suggestion he hears rather than a pretence. Effort is
  the model's own number under a daily ceiling. A note is a draft that
  waits for his yes; the form goes through the door it always did; a page
  read and a workspace file are reversible and in the autonomy ledger. An
  employer's reply now lands on the application (`replied`, `rejected`) and
  on the opportunity as evidence, which makes it due at once — and what
  happened across opportunities is handed to the next pass as sentences,
  never as rules. The words that make an opportunity a job live in the
  adapter and the evidence; the pursuit module itself carries none of them
  (held by `tests/test_studies.py`).
  Seen on his machine, sandboxed with rehearsal on, one real application
  (submitted two days earlier), two passes through the frontier at ~130 s
  each: the first read the record's "1 field left as a question" as a
  possible disqualifier and told HIM to go and look — so the evidence now
  names the open questions, and the brief says she does the work or says
  plainly it needs him. The second pass filed a suggestion (get conversant
  with their product before a screening call — his to do) and a wait of
  five days for their screening to run, and judged it worth no minutes of
  her own today. No note, no document, no noise: the brief's "another is
  left alone after applying" happened on its own. Still to see: the first
  outcome recorded from a real reply, and a pass that chooses a note or a
  document for a real opportunity.
